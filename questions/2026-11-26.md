## Q1. Mathematical Physics — Symmetric-matrix diagonalization

**Practice style:** JAM / GATE

**Question.** A = [[5,3],[3,5]]. Find both eigenvalues and normalized eigenvectors.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

First calculate det(A - λI) = (5 - λ)^2 - 3^2 = 0. Therefore λ = 5+3 = 8 or 5-3 = 2. The eigenvector for the larger eigenvalue is (1,1)/sqrt(2); the smaller has (1,-1)/sqrt(2). These vectors are orthogonal and unit length.

</details>

## Q2. Classical Mechanics — Stability of a central-force orbit

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** For V(r)=-k/r, m=k=1 in consistent units and angular momentum L=5, find the circular-orbit radius and determine radial stability.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

The effective potential is V_eff=L²/(2mr²)-k/r. Differentiate: V_eff'=-L²/(mr³)+k/r². At its stationary point r0=L²/(mk)=5²=**25**. Next V_eff''(r0)=k/r0³>0; the effective potential has a minimum, so small radial disturbances oscillate rather than grow.

</details>

## Q3. Electromagnetic Theory — Dielectric inserted at fixed charge

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** An isolated charged parallel-plate capacitor initially stores U0=15 mJ. A dielectric with relative permittivity k=3 completely fills the gap. Find the new energy, assuming no charge leaks.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Isolated means Q is fixed. With dielectric C'=kC, U'=Q²/(2C')=U0/k=15/3=**5.0 mJ**. Warning: fixed voltage would give a different energy change.

</details>

## Q4. Quantum Mechanics — Infinite square well energy

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** A particle is in the nth eigenstate n=2 of a one-dimensional infinite square well. Find E_n/E_1.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Solve the boundary-value problem ψ(0)=ψ(L)=0; allowed wave numbers are k_n=n*pi/L. E_n=ħ²k_n²/(2m), so E_n/E_1=n²=2²=**4**. It is the boundary condition, not the classical potential energy, that quantizes E.

</details>

## Q5. Statistical Physics — Entropy from multiplicity

**Practice style:** CSIR

**Question.** A system has N=8 independent, equally probable two-state degrees of freedom. Find the entropy in units of kB.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

The multiplicity is Omega=2^N=2^8. Boltzmann entropy S=kB ln(Omega)=kB ln(2^8)=**8 kB ln 2**. Assumes all 2^N states are accessible and equiprobable.

</details>

## Q6. Optics — Brewster angle

**Practice style:** JAM

**Question.** Light travels from n1=1 into a medium of refractive index n2=sqrt(3). Find the Brewster angle.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

For nonmagnetic lossless media, tan(theta_B)=n2/n1. Here tan(theta_B)=sqrt(3), hence theta_B=**60 degrees**. At this incidence, the reflected p-polarized amplitude vanishes.

</details>

## Q7. Atomic Physics — Hydrogen photon emission

**Practice style:** GATE / CSIR

**Question.** A hydrogen electron transitions from n_i=5 to n_f=2. Find emitted photon energy in eV (ignore fine structure).

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Hydrogen bound-state energies are E_n=-13.6/n² eV. E_gamma=E_i-E_f=13.6(1/2²-1/5²)=**2.8560 eV**. Emission energy is positive because the final bound level is lower.

</details>

## Q8. Solid State — Bragg diffraction

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** First-order X-ray diffraction occurs from lattice planes with d=0.20 nm using wavelength λ=0.20 nm. Find the Bragg angle θ.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Bragg's law is nλ=2d sinθ. With n=1 and λ=d, sinθ=1/2, so **θ=30 degrees**. The measured diffractometer scattering angle is often 2θ=60 degrees.

</details>

## Q9. Nuclear Physics — Mass defect and Q-value

**Practice style:** JAM / CSIR

**Question.** An exothermic nuclear reaction has initial-minus-final mass defect Δm=0.006 u. Find the released energy in MeV using 1 u*c²=931.5 MeV.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Energy conservation relates mass loss to kinetic/radiation energy via Q=Δm c². Q=0.006*931.5=**5.5890 MeV**. The positive sign denotes energy release.

</details>

## Q10. Electronics and Experimental Physics — Parallel resistors

**Practice style:** GATE / JAM

**Question.** Two ideal resistors of R1=4 ohm and R2=6 ohm are parallel. Find their equivalent resistance.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Current adds across equal voltage: 1/R_eq=1/R1+1/R2. Therefore R_eq=R1 R2/(R1+R2)=4*6/(4+6)=**2.40 ohm**. It must be below the smaller branch resistance.

</details>

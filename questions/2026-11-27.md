## Q1. Mathematical Physics — Vector divergence

**Practice style:** JAM / GATE

**Question.** For F = (3x^2, 3xy, 2z^2), evaluate div F at (x,y,z)=(1,0,1).

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Use div F = ∂F_x/∂x + ∂F_y/∂y + ∂F_z/∂z = 6x + 3x + 4z = 9x + 4z. Substitution gives (9)(1) + (4)(1) = **13**. The derivative of xy with respect to y is x, not y.

</details>

## Q2. Classical Mechanics — Conservation of mechanical energy

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** A particle slides without friction from rest through a vertical drop h=45 m. With g=10 m/s², what is its speed at the bottom?

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Mechanical energy conservation gives m*g*h=(1/2)m*v². Thus v=sqrt(2gh)=sqrt(2*10*45)=**30 m/s**. The mass cancels; this holds only when nonconservative work is absent.

</details>

## Q3. Electromagnetic Theory — Vacuum EM wave

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** A vacuum plane wave has peak electric field E0=12 V/m. Find its B-field amplitude; use c=3.0e8 m/s.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Maxwell's equations imply E0=c B0 for a plane wave in vacuum. Thus B0=12/(3.0e8)=**4.00e-08 T**. E, B and the propagation direction are mutually perpendicular.

</details>

## Q4. Quantum Mechanics — Quantum potential step; flux ratio

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** A particle of energy E>V0 encounters a 1D upward step. If transmitted and incident wave numbers satisfy k2/k1=3/4, find reflection and transmission probabilities.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Continuity of ψ and ψ' gives r=(k1-k2)/(k1+k2). Here r=(1-3/4)/(1+3/4)=1/7. So R=|r|²=**1/49** (≈0.0204), T=1-R=**0.9796**. Probability transmission includes the velocity/flux factor, unlike |t|² alone.

</details>

## Q5. Statistical Physics — Equipartition and heat capacity

**Practice style:** CSIR

**Question.** Find the internal energy and constant-volume heat capacity for 1 moles of a classical monatomic ideal gas.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Each atom has three translational quadratic degrees of freedom. U=(3/2)nRT=**1.5 RT**, so C_V=(∂U/∂T)_V=**1.5 R**. Rotations and vibrations of structureless monatomic particles do not contribute.

</details>

## Q6. Optics — Polarization: Malus' law

**Practice style:** JAM

**Question.** Linearly polarized light of intensity I0 passes through an analyzer whose axis is 45 degrees to the input polarization. Find I/I0.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Malus' law gives I/I0=cos²(45 degrees)=**1/2**. A perpendicular analyzer blocks the ideal beam; a parallel analyzer transmits its full intensity.

</details>

## Q7. Atomic Physics — Angular momentum eigenvalues

**Practice style:** GATE / CSIR

**Question.** For a simultaneous eigenstate |l,m> with l=2, m=1, give L² and Lz eigenvalues in ħ units.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

The defining eigenvalue equations are L²|l,m>=ħ² l(l+1)|l,m> and Lz|l,m>=ħ m|l,m>. So **L²=6 ħ²** and **Lz=1 ħ**. Note the magnitude squared is not (Lz)².

</details>

## Q8. Solid State — Fermi energy density scaling

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** The number density of a three-dimensional free-electron gas increases by a factor of 27. By what factor does its Fermi energy change?

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

For a spin-1/2 3D free-electron gas, k_F is proportional to n^(1/3), so E_F=ħ²k_F²/(2m) is proportional to n^(2/3). The factor is 27^(2/3)=**9**.

</details>

## Q9. Nuclear Physics — Radioactive decay

**Practice style:** JAM / CSIR

**Question.** A sample has half-life 3 days. What fraction remains after 6 days?

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

N(t)=N0*2^(-t/T_half). Here t/T_half=6/3=2. The surviving fraction is **1/4**. This is the number of undecayed nuclei, not the fraction that decayed.

</details>

## Q10. Electronics and Experimental Physics — Ideal inverting op-amp

**Practice style:** GATE / JAM

**Question.** An ideal inverting op-amp has Rin=1 kilo-ohm and Rf=4 kilo-ohm. With Vin=0.5 V, find the closed-loop gain and Vout.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

For negative feedback, Vout/Vin=-Rf/Rin=-4/1=**-4**. Thus Vout=-4*0.5=**-2.00 V**. This presumes sufficient output headroom and an ideal op-amp.

</details>

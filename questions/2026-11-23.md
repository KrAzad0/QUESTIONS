## Q1 — Linear algebra (JAM/GATE)

For the real matrix \(A=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix}\), find its eigenvalues and normalized eigenvectors.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

\(\det(A-\lambda I)=(3-\lambda)^2-4=(\lambda-5)(\lambda-1)=0.\)
So \(\lambda_1=5\), \(\lambda_2=1\). The corresponding eigenvectors are proportional to \((1,1)^T\) and \((1,-1)^T\), respectively. Normalizing:

\[
v_1=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v_2=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**Check:** \(v_1\cdot v_2=0\), consistent with real symmetric matrices.

</details>

## Q2 — Vector calculus (JAM)

For \(\mathbf F=(x^2,xy,z^2)\), compute \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\) at \((1,0,2)\).

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

\[
\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=\frac{\partial x^2}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial(xy)}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial z^2}{\partial z}
=2x+x+2z=3x+2z.
\]
At \((1,0,2)\): \(3(1)+2(2)=\boxed7\). Note that the divergence need not depend on \(y\).

</details>

## Q3 — Differential equations and damping (GATE)

Solve \(\ddot x+4\dot x+13x=0\) for real-valued \(x(t)\).

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Try \(x=e^{rt}\), yielding \(r^2+4r+13=0\), so \(r=-2\pm 3i\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{x(t)=e^{-2t}(C_1\cos3t+C_2\sin3t)}.
\]
The real negative part gives exponential damping, while the imaginary part gives angular frequency \(3\).

</details>

## Q4 — Effective potential and orbit stability (TIFR)

A particle of mass \(m\) moves under \(V(r)=-k/r\) with fixed angular momentum \(L\). Find the radius of a circular orbit and show that the orbit is stable to small radial disturbances.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

\[
V_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{k}{r},\quad
\frac{dV_{\rm eff}}{dr}=-\frac{L^2}{mr^3}+\frac{k}{r^2}=0.
\]
Thus \(\boxed{r_0=L^2/(mk)}\). Differentiate again:

\[
V_{\rm eff}''(r)=\frac{3L^2}{mr^4}-\frac{2k}{r^3},
\quad
V_{\rm eff}''(r_0)=\frac{k}{r_0^3}>0.
\]
Hence \(r_0\) is a local minimum: the circular orbit is radially stable. This uses \(k>0\) and \(L\ne0\).

</details>

## Q5 — Electromagnetic waves (GATE)

A plane EM wave in vacuum has electric-field amplitude \(E_0=3\ \mathrm{V/m}\). Find the magnetic-field amplitude.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

From Maxwell's equations for a vacuum plane wave, \(E_0=cB_0\), hence

\[
B_0=\frac{3}{3.0\times10^8}=\boxed{1.0\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm T}.
\]
**Check:** Electric and magnetic fields are transverse, in phase and mutually perpendicular.

</details>

## Q6 — Quantum step potential (TIFR)

A 1D particle approaches a step from \(V=0\) to \(V=V_0\) with incident energy \(E=4V_0/3\). Find the reflection and transmission *probabilities*.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

With equal mass on both sides, \(k_1\propto\sqrt{E}\) and \(k_2\propto\sqrt{E-V_0}\). Then

\[
\frac{k_2}{k_1}=\sqrt{\frac{E-V_0}{E}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}}=\frac12.
\]
Continuity of \(\psi\) and \(\psi'\) gives \(r=(k_1-k_2)/(k_1+k_2)=1/3\). Consequently,

\[
\boxed{R=|r|^2=\frac19},\qquad \boxed{T=1-R=\frac89}.
\]
The transmitted **amplitude squared** alone is not the transmission probability; the probability-current velocity ratio is required.

</details>

## Q7 — Quantum harmonic oscillator (JAM)

Determine \(\Delta x\Delta p\) for the harmonic oscillator ground state.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

For \(n=0\), \(\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle=0\), \(\langle x^2\rangle=\hbar/(2m\omega)\), \(\langle p^2\rangle=m\hbar\omega/2\). So

\[
\Delta x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},\quad
\Delta p=\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}},\quad
\boxed{\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar/2}.
\]
The ground state saturates the Heisenberg lower bound.

</details>

## Q8 — Two-level canonical ensemble (CSIR)

Nondegenerate levels have energies \(0\) and \(\Delta\). At a temperature with \(e^{-\beta\Delta}=1/3\), determine the excited-state probability and average energy.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

\[
Z=1+e^{-\beta\Delta}=\frac43.
\]
Then \(P_{\rm excited}=e^{-\beta\Delta}/Z=(1/3)/(4/3)=\boxed{1/4}\), and

\[
\boxed{\langle E\rangle=\Delta P_{\rm excited}=\Delta/4}.
\]
The answer lies between 0 and \(\Delta\), as a physical mean energy must.

</details>

## Q9 — Double-slit optics (GATE)

Light of wavelength \(600\ \mathrm{nm}\) falls on two narrow slits separated by \(0.30\ \mathrm{mm}\). The screen is \(2.0\ \mathrm m\) away. Find the small-angle fringe separation.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

\[
\beta=\frac{\lambda D}{d}=\frac{(600\times10^{-9})(2.0)}{0.30\times10^{-3}}
=0.004\ \mathrm m=\boxed{4.0\ \mathrm{mm}}.
\]
This assumes \(D\gg d\) and small observation angles.

</details>

## Q10 — Density of states and low-temperature heat capacity (CSIR)

In a three-dimensional Debye solid, the acoustic phonon density of states behaves as \(g(\omega)\propto\omega^2\). Explain why the low-temperature heat capacity scales as \(T^3\).

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Thermal phonon energy (excluding temperature-independent zero-point energy) is

\[
U(T)\propto\int_0^{\omega_D}\!d\omega\,
\frac{\hbar\omega\,\omega^2}{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}.
\]
At \(T\ll\Theta_D\), replace the high-frequency cutoff by infinity. With \(x=\hbar\omega/(k_BT)\) the integral becomes

\[
U(T)\propto\frac{(k_BT)^4}{\hbar^3}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3\,dx}{e^x-1}\propto T^4.
\]
Therefore \(\boxed{C_V=\partial U/\partial T\propto T^3}\). The essential physics is the **three-dimensional** phonon mode count together with Bose occupation.

</details>

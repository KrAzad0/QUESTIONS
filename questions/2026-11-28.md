## Q1. Mathematical Physics — Gaussian integral

**Practice style:** JAM / GATE

**Question.** Evaluate I = integral from -infinity to infinity of exp(-25 x^2) dx.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Start with the known Gaussian integral ∫exp(-u²)du=sqrt(pi). Set u=5x, so dx=du/5. Thus I=sqrt(pi)/5. Dimensional check: if x has dimensions of length, the coefficient of x² must have reciprocal-length-squared units.

</details>

## Q2. Classical Mechanics — Atwood machine

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** Two ideal masses m1=1 kg and m2=7 kg hang over a frictionless massless pulley. Find acceleration as a multiple of g and the string tension as a multiple of g (in kg*g units).

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Write m2*g - T = m2*a and T - m1*g = m1*a. Adding gives a=(7-1)/(7+1) g = **6/8 g**. Insert into T=m1(g+a) to get T=2*m1*m2/(m1+m2) g = **14/8 g**. Check the heavier mass accelerates downward with a<g.

</details>

## Q3. Electromagnetic Theory — Series capacitance

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** Two ideal capacitors C1=4 microfarad and C2=6 microfarad are in series. Determine C_eq.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

In series, 1/C_eq=1/C1+1/C2. Equivalently, C_eq=C1*C2/(C1+C2)=(4*6)/(4+6) = **2.40 microfarad**. The equivalent is less than the smaller capacitor.

</details>

## Q4. Quantum Mechanics — Harmonic oscillator uncertainties

**Practice style:** TIFR / CSIR

**Question.** For the nth stationary oscillator state with n=4, express Δx Δp in multiples of ħ.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

For each number state, <x>=<p>=0, <x²>=(n+1/2)ħ/(mω), and <p²>=(n+1/2)mħω. Therefore Δx Δp=(n+1/2)ħ=**4.5 ħ**. The ground state n=0 saturates Heisenberg; excited stationary states do not.

</details>

## Q5. Statistical Physics — Two-level canonical probabilities

**Practice style:** CSIR

**Question.** A nondegenerate two-level system has energies 0 and Delta. At temperature T, exp(-Delta/(kB T))=1/4. Find the excited probability and mean energy.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Z=1+exp(-beta Delta)=1+1/4=(5)/4. P_exc=(1/4)/Z=**1/5**. Mean U=0*P_ground+Delta*P_exc=**Delta/5**. Partition functions normalize Boltzmann weights.

</details>

## Q6. Optics — Young double-slit interference

**Practice style:** JAM

**Question.** For wavelength 700 nm, screen distance 3 m, and slit separation 0.4 mm, find the small-angle fringe spacing in mm.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

For d << D, β=λD/d. Converting all dimensions, β=700*3*0.001/0.4 mm=**5.250 mm**. Fringe separation grows with wavelength and distance, decreases with slit spacing.

</details>

## Q7. Atomic Physics — Electric-dipole selection rules

**Practice style:** GATE / CSIR

**Question.** Is the atomic electric-dipole transition (l=2,m=0) to (l=1,m=1) allowed by orbital angular-momentum selection rules?

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

E1 transitions have Δl=±1 and Δm=0,±1 (subject to additional state restrictions). Here Δl=-1 and Δm=+1: **allowed by these selection rules**.

</details>

## Q8. Solid State — Nearest-neighbor spacing

**Practice style:** GATE

**Question.** A SC cubic crystal has lattice parameter a=0.30 nm. Find its nearest-neighbor distance.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

The nearest-neighbor geometric relation for SC is d=a. Numerically, d=**0.3000 nm**. Use cube edges (SC), body diagonal (BCC), or face diagonal (FCC), respectively.

</details>

## Q9. Nuclear Physics — Nuclear radius scaling

**Practice style:** JAM / CSIR

**Question.** Two nuclei have mass numbers A and 64A. Find the ratio of their radii R2/R1.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

Empirically R≈r0*A^(1/3), reflecting approximately constant nuclear density. Hence R2/R1=(64A/A)^(1/3)=**4**.

</details>

## Q10. Electronics and Experimental Physics — RC time constant

**Practice style:** GATE / JAM

**Question.** A simple RC charging circuit has R=9 kilo-ohm and C=9 microfarad. Find the time constant in milliseconds.

<details><summary><strong>Show worked solution</strong></summary>

τ=RC. Since (1 kilo-ohm)(1 microfarad)=1 millisecond, τ=9*9=**81 ms**. Charging from zero reaches 1-e^(-1)≈63.2% of the final capacitor voltage at t=τ.

</details>

## 2. Conceptual dependency order — learn in this sequence

1. **Mathematical methods:** units/dimensions and errors; differential/vector calculus; Gauss/Stokes; matrices, Hermitian/eigenvalue problems; ODE/PDE; Fourier transforms; complex integration and residues; probability; coordinate systems.
2. **Classical mechanics:** Newtonian dynamics and constraints; work/energy; central forces and effective potential; rigid body; oscillations and normal modes; Lagrangian/Hamiltonian formulations; small oscillations; relativity.
3. **Electricity and magnetism:** Gauss and Coulomb; Laplace/Poisson and boundary values; dielectrics; magnetostatics, Faraday; Maxwell equations; waves, Poynting, reflection/transmission.
4. **Quantum mechanics:** states and Born rule; operators/eigenvalues; 1D wells/barriers; SHO (differential and ladder routes); angular momentum/spin; central potentials/hydrogen; approximation methods, identical particles and scattering.
5. **Thermo/statistical:** laws; thermodynamic potentials and Maxwell relations; microstates/entropy; canonical/grand canonical ensembles; MB/BE/FD; quantum gases and partition functions.
6. **Oscillations/waves/optics:** damped-driven modes, resonance; interference, diffraction; polarization, coherence; optical instruments.
7. **Atomic/molecular:** hydrogen spectra; fine and hyperfine structure; Zeeman/Stark effects; angular momentum addition, selection rules; vibrational/rotational spectra.
8. **Solid state:** lattice and reciprocal lattice; diffraction; phonons; free electrons and bands; semiconductors, dielectric/magnetic response, superconductivity.
9. **Nuclear/particle:** binding, decay and nuclear models; nuclear reactions; elementary particle families and symmetries.
10. **Electronics/experimental:** analog circuits, op-amps, diodes/transistors; digital logic; error propagation, fitting and instrumentation.
11. **General aptitude:** arithmetic, logic, reading, data interpretation; especially for GATE (15 marks) and NET A.

**Depth rule:** Know a definition → derive the formula → calculate an example → test limiting cases → solve a cross-topic challenge → revisit after 2/7 days.

## 6. Example conceptual chains (TIFR/NET problem-solving)

- Symmetric matrix → orthonormal eigenvectors → Hermitian operator → quantum measurements.
- Effective potential → stable orbit → small oscillation → linearization/eigenfrequencies.
- Fourier transform → wave packet → group velocity → uncertainty principle.
- Maxwell boundary conditions → optics reflection/transmission → energy flux.
- Partition function → mean energy → heat capacity → Einstein/Debye solid.
- Rotational symmetry → angular-momentum commutators → selection rules.

**For each chain:** write a one-page derivation, a dimensional check, one intuitive explanation, 5 standard PYQs and 2 unfamiliar variations.

## 8. Mock strategy

Suggested *minimum cumulative targets*: TIFR 5 full papers (historical/official mock format), NET 5, GATE PH 8 and JAM PH 8, while preserving time for analysis. After each mock record attempted/accurate marks by topic, lost marks by error type, questions skipped, and time per section.

For TIFR A and NET/GATE MCQs, calculate expected value rather than using a fixed "attempt everything" rule; no-negative TIFR B rewards attempts. For JAM MSQ, all correct selections matter. For numerical answers, check units and reasonable orders of magnitude.

## 9. Diagnostic baseline on 23–30 November

1. Solve one **TIFR 2026 Physics** paper.
2. Attempt one **NET Physical Sciences** past paper or official model question paper.
3. Attempt 30–40 mixed GATE/JAM questions.
4. Make a **red/amber/green** topic map: red = unable to start, amber = method known but error-prone, green = can solve independently.
5. Prioritize amber→green first, then high-yield red subjects. Do not neglect easy/fast marks from aptitude and electronics.
